The person behind the institution

A commitment to education, capability and institution building

Dr. Iman Khan is the Founder and Managing Director of Chandidas Institute of Nursing and Paramedical Science (CINPS), an institute based in Nanoor, Birbhum, West Bengal. His work is centred on building an educational institution where learning is connected with practical competence, professional conduct and preparation for real workplace responsibilities.

His approach to education places importance on more than the completion of a syllabus. It emphasises conceptual understanding, procedure-oriented practical learning, continuous assessment, discipline, communication and the habits required in professional healthcare environments. Through CINPS, he seeks to make structured paramedical education more accessible to learners, including students from smaller towns and rural communities.

Academic background

Education and intellectual development

Dr. Khan holds a Bachelor of Arts (Honours) in History. His academic background in the humanities informs a broad interest in how people understand the world, how knowledge is communicated, and how social and institutional systems shape learning.

In 2022, he received an Honorary Doctorate Award from Magic Book of Record. The award plaque identifies his contribution in the fields of entrepreneurship, business and social work. This is an honorary recognition and is distinct from an earned PhD, medical doctorate or clinical qualification.

Credential transparency: The honorary doctorate is presented here using the wording visible on the supplied award plaque. It is not represented as a government-accredited academic degree or a professional healthcare licence.

Institutional leadership

Building CINPS around practical, skill-focused education

As Founder and Managing Director, Dr. Khan's institutional focus is on developing an academic environment that connects classroom learning with practical skill development. CINPS describes its educational framework through the following components:

Concept-based learning

Build subject understanding before expecting students to perform procedures or solve workplace problems.

Procedure-oriented practice

Give importance to practical demonstrations, supervised skill practice and correct procedural habits.

Continuous assessment

Use revision, feedback and performance checks to identify learning gaps and strengthen competence.

Professional behaviour

Encourage discipline, communication, documentation, punctuality and responsible conduct.

From education to workplace readiness

Healthcare support roles require more than theoretical familiarity. Students must understand their responsibilities, follow instructions, maintain professional boundaries, communicate clearly and work carefully under supervision. CINPS aims to make these expectations part of the learning process.

The institute also describes internship exposure and placement assistance as elements of its student support. Internship arrangements, eligibility and placement outcomes depend on the applicable course, institutional arrangements and the requirements of the receiving organisation. Students are encouraged to verify course-specific details before admission.

Research and scholarship

Research interests: language, cognition and institutions

Alongside his institutional responsibilities, Dr. Iman Khan pursues independent research into the relationship between human cognition, language, social practice and institutional recognition. His work explores how people may understand or practise something before a stable term for it becomes widely established, and how institutions influence which ideas become formally recognised and communicated.

Research paper · 2026

Why Certain Concepts Appear Late in Human Language: A Cognitive–Institutional Perspective

A study of the relationship between cognitive understanding, social relevance, communication and institutional recognition.

The paper proposes that concepts do not become linguistically visible only because people can perceive or understand them. Their wider naming and recognition may also depend on repeated communication, social relevance and institutional endorsement. It distinguishes cognitive capacity from formal linguistic encoding and discusses implications for education and knowledge transmission.

Author: Iman Khan  ·  Written: 9 January 2026  ·  SSRN posting: 5 February 2026  ·  Zenodo: Version 1, 2 February 2026

Areas of research interest

  • Cognitive linguistics: how language relates to human thought and conceptual understanding.
  • Language evolution: how expressions and concepts emerge, change and become established.
  • Institutional language: how formal systems influence terminology, recognition and knowledge sharing.
  • Education and cognition: the relationship between vocabulary, understanding and learning.

The paper is a theoretical contribution and should be read as the author's research argument, not as a clinical or experimental finding. The public research record is available through the repository links above.

Vision and values

A long-term vision for accessible and responsible healthcare education

Dr. Khan's vision is to contribute to a skilled healthcare support workforce by helping make quality-oriented paramedical education more accessible, practical and responsive to the needs of learners and healthcare workplaces.

He believes that a credible education system should combine academic foundations with supervised practical exposure, ethical conduct, clear communication and respect for professional responsibilities. It should also communicate honestly with students about course recognition, eligibility, training arrangements and career pathways.

“Focus on skill development, practical knowledge and discipline. A strong foundation in education can help create better long-term opportunities.”— Dr. Iman Khan

This statement expresses the educational values represented in this profile.
