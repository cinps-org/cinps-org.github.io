Radiology & Imaging Technology Course After 12th: 2026–27 Student Guide

Understand the course, eligibility, clinical training and career pathways

Course Guide · Academic Session 2026–27

Published: 19 November 2026 · CINPS Editorial Team

Medical imaging helps healthcare professionals investigate injuries and diseases, monitor treatment and make clinical decisions. X-ray, computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and other imaging methods are used in hospitals and diagnostic facilities. Behind these services are trained imaging professionals who prepare equipment, support patients, follow safety procedures and work with authorised clinicians.

If you are considering a Radiology and Imaging Technology course after Class 12, it is important to understand the different qualifications available, the practical training involved and the rules that apply to the programme you choose. This guide explains the subject in clear terms and highlights important 2026–27 developments.

Important distinction: Course titles such as Diploma in Medical Radiology and Imaging Technology (D.MRIT), Diploma in Radiography (Diagnostic) and other radiology or X-ray diplomas may belong to different course frameworks. Do not assume they are interchangeable. Verify the exact qualification, awarding body, institution approval and current admission rules.

1. What is Medical Radiology and Imaging Technology?

Medical Radiology and Imaging Technology is an allied healthcare field concerned with producing diagnostic images using specialised equipment. Imaging professionals work as part of a clinical team and follow established protocols for patient identification, preparation, positioning, radiation protection where relevant, equipment operation within their authorised scope, image quality and documentation.

The work requires technical knowledge as well as communication, patient care, attention to detail and a strong commitment to safety. Imaging professionals do not independently diagnose patients or replace the role of radiologists and other authorised medical practitioners.

2. What imaging technologies may students study?

  • Conventional X-ray: Uses ionising radiation to create images of internal structures, commonly bones and the chest.
  • Computed Tomography (CT): Uses X-rays and computer processing to create cross-sectional images.
  • Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI): Uses magnetic fields and radiofrequency signals to create detailed images; it does not use ionising radiation.
  • Ultrasound: Uses high-frequency sound waves to produce images of structures inside the body.
  • Fluoroscopy: Produces moving X-ray images for selected diagnostic and interventional procedures.

Not every diploma programme provides the same depth of training or authorises students to operate every modality. Check the official syllabus and actual clinical training arrangements before enrolling.

3. What is the D.MRIT course?

The National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions (NCAHP) notified a competency-based curriculum for the Diploma in Medical Radiology and Imaging Technology (D.MRIT) in September 2026. A Ministry of Health and Family Welfare announcement dated 7 October 2026 states that the programme is two years long and includes a six-month clinical internship in radiology and imaging.

The same announcement states that institutions must adopt the curriculum from academic year 2027–28, while institutions wishing to implement it earlier may adopt it from 2026–27. Students should therefore ask each institution which curriculum applies to their batch and obtain the relevant written documents.

4. Eligibility: can you study radiology after Class 12?

For the NCAHP D.MRIT programme described in the Ministry announcement, the stated entry requirement is completion of Class 12 in the Science stream with Physics, Chemistry and Biology (PCB) from a recognised board or equivalent.

Other radiography or imaging qualifications may have their own eligibility conditions. For example, the State Medical Faculty of West Bengal lists a Diploma in Radiography (Diagnostic) among its courses. Do not transfer the D.MRIT eligibility rule to another programme without checking that programme's official prospectus.

  • Confirm the exact qualification title.
  • Check accepted Class 12 subjects and board requirements.
  • Confirm minimum marks, age criteria and selection process, if any.
  • Ask for the current academic-session prospectus or official admission notice.

5. Course duration and clinical training

The notified NCAHP D.MRIT programme is two years and includes a six-month clinical internship, according to the Ministry's October 2026 announcement. Clinical training matters because learners need supervised exposure to patient preparation, imaging department workflow, equipment safety, communication and quality procedures.

West Bengal's State Medical Faculty separately lists Diploma in Radiography (Diagnostic), with a published course duration of two years plus six months of compulsory post-examination training. This is a distinct published course pathway; students should verify the current prospectus, affiliation and rules for the specific institution and intake.

Before admission: Ask where clinical training takes place, who supervises students, how attendance and competencies are documented, which imaging modalities are available for training and whether the internship or post-examination training is compulsory under your programme.

6. Skills that matter in imaging departments

  • Understanding of anatomy, medical terminology and imaging principles.
  • Careful patient identification, communication and preparation.
  • Knowledge of radiation protection and safety procedures relevant to the modality.
  • Attention to image quality, equipment checks and departmental protocols.
  • Professional conduct, confidentiality and accurate documentation.
  • Ability to work within a multidisciplinary clinical team and follow supervision.

7. Career pathways after a radiology qualification

Depending on the exact qualification, registration requirements, practical competence and employer criteria, graduates may explore roles such as radiography or imaging technologist, X-ray department technician, CT or MRI department support roles, or other imaging-service positions for which they meet the stated requirements.

Some specialised roles require additional training, experience, registration or employer-specific competencies. A course does not guarantee a job, a particular salary or eligibility for every imaging role. Always compare your qualification with the requirements in an actual recruitment notification.

9. Frequently asked questions

Is radiology a good career option after 12th?

It may suit students interested in medical technology, patient interaction, imaging equipment and detail-oriented clinical work. Suitability depends on the student's interests, the qualification selected and the applicable professional requirements.

Is D.MRIT a two-year course?

The NCAHP D.MRIT programme described in the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare announcement is two years and includes a six-month clinical internship. Check the current official curriculum and implementation instructions for your admission batch.

Can a student from the Arts or Commerce stream apply?

The Ministry announcement specifies Class 12 Science with Physics, Chemistry and Biology for the D.MRIT programme. Other radiology-related courses may have different rules, so verify the exact course rather than assuming eligibility.

Are X-ray, CT and MRI the same?

No. They are different imaging methods with different equipment, operating principles and safety considerations. Training and authorisation for one modality should not be assumed to cover another.

Does a radiology diploma guarantee employment?

No. Employment depends on qualification validity, applicable registration or recruitment rules, practical skills, vacancies and employer selection criteria.
